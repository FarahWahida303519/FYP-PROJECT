1.0 INTRODUCTION 
 
 
The Malay language consists of various regional dialects that reflect linguistic diversity, 
cultural identity and community interaction. The Kedah Malay dialect contains distinctive 
variations in vocabulary and pronunciation. Previous studies have examined  vowel-
lengthening processes in Hulu Sik (Norfazila  Ab. Hamid & Rahim Aman, 2014) and lexical 
comprehension among speakers in Pulau Tuba and Satun (Harishon Radzi et al., 2018). 
However, these studies focus on specific linguistic characteristics, highlighting the opportunity 
for continuous digital document ation of Kedah Malay vocabulary, contextual meanings and 
authentic pronunciation recordings. 
 
Digital platforms provide opportunities for collecting and preserving linguistic information 
through community participation (Androutsopoulos , 2014). Crowdsourcing enables local 
speakers to contribute language data including text and audio recordings through accessible 
digital platforms (Ardila et al., 2020; Leemann et al., 2016). Nevertheless, community generate 
data can contain inconsistencies that require appropriate verification. Advances in Artificial 
Intelligence (AI) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) offer potential approaches for 
processing linguistic information through pretrained language models and few  shot learning 
(Devlin et al., 2019; Brown et al., 2020). However, underrepresented language varieties face 
challenges because linguistic resources are unevenly distributed across languages (Joshi et al., 
2020). 
 
To address these challenges, Loghat Kami is proposed as a web -based platform under the 
project Voices of the North  that integrates multimodal crowdsourcing, AI -assisted screening 
and language expert verification to document and preserve the Kedah Malay dialect. 
Community members can contribute dialect vocabulary, contextual meanings, example 
sentences and audio pronunciation recordings. The AI component will screen submissions for 
spelling, pronunciation and language use  meanwhile language experts make the final 
verification decisions. Verified contributions will be organized into a structured digital dialect 
corpus to support linguistic preservation, learning, future research and language -based 
applications.

2.0 PREVIOUS WORKS 
 
Various digital platforms and linguistic resources have been developed to support language 
documentation, translation and linguistic research. These platforms adopt different approaches, 
including institutional dictionaries, digital language corpora, community based contributions 
and Artificial Intelligence (AI)-assisted language processing. Existing platforms such as Pusat 
Rujukan Persuratan Melayu (PRPM), Kamus Dewan Perdana, the SEAlang Library, Glosbe 
and ChatGPT provide different functionalities for managing linguistic information. 
 
 
2.1 Pusat Rujukan Persuratan Melayu 
 
 
Figure 2.1: Pusat Rujukan Persuratan Melayu Home Interface 
 
Pusat Rujukan Persuratan Melayu (PRPM) is an online linguistic reference portal maintained 
by Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka (DBP). The platform provides various Malay language 
resources including dictionary definitions, terminology, linguistic references and regional 
dialect glossaries. One of its resources is the Glosari Dialek Kedah which documents Kedah 
Malay vocabulary through phonetic transcription, word classification, meanings, example 
sentences and standard Malay equivalents. 
 
However, PRPM primarily functions as a language reference portal rather than a dedicated 
platform for continuous community -based collection of multimodal dialect contributions. In

comparison, the proposed Loghat Kami system aims to allow local speakers to contribute 
Kedah Malay vocabulary, contextual meanings and audio pronunciation recordings through a 
structured submission process. These contributions will undergo AI -assisted preliminary 
screening and final language expert verification before being accepted into the verified dialect 
corpus. 
 
 
2.2  Kamus Dewan Perdana (DBP) 
  
 
Figure 2.2: Kamus Dewan Perdana Home Interface 
 
Kamus Dewan Perdana is a comprehensive Malay language dictionary published by Dewan 
Bahasa dan Pustaka (DBP). It contains extensive linguistic information including word 
definitions, pronunciation, grammatical classification, usage examples, etymological 
information and vocabulary from regional dialects. The dictionary serves as an authoritative 
reference for understanding Malay vocabulary and its usage across different linguistic contexts. 
Although Kamus Dewan Perdana provides comprehensive linguistic documentation, its 
primary purpose is to serve as a reference dictionary rather than a platform for continuous 
community-based dialect data collection. The proposed Loghat Kami system adopts a different 
approach by encouraging local communities to contribute dialect expressions, meanings and 
pronunciation recordings directly. Nevertheless, Kamus Dewan Perdana provides a useful 
reference for organizing linguistic entries and presenting accurate vocabulary information 
within a structured digital corpus.

2.3 The SEAlang Library 
 
 
Figure 2.3: The SEAlang Library Home Interface 
 
 
The SEAlang Library is a digital linguistic resource that provides access to dictionaries and 
language data for Southeast Asian languages. Its Malay language resources include searchable 
linguistic references and text -based information that support language learning,  comparative 
linguistic studies and academic research. The platform demonstrates how digital resources can 
improve the accessibility and organization of linguistic information. 
 
However, the Malay resources examined in this review primarily emphasize linguistic 
reference and information retrieval rather than a dedicated system for continuously collecting 
and verifying community -generated Kedah Malay dialect data. In comparison, Lo ghat Kami 
aims to support community contributions in multiple formats  including text and audio  
meanwhile incorporating AI -assisted preliminary screening and expert verification. This 
approach is intended to support the development of a structured and continuously expandable 
Kedah Malay dialect corpus.

2.4 Glosbe 
 
 
 
Figure 2.4: Glosbe Home Interface 
 
Glosbe is a multilingual online dictionary that provides translations, word meanings, example 
sentences and pronunciation resources. It also allows users to contribute linguistic information, 
including translations for the Malay–Kedah Malay language pair. 
 
However, community contributions may contain inconsistencies that affect the accuracy and 
reliability of linguistic information. Previous studies highlight the importance of quality control 
in crowdsourced language data (Ardila et al., 2020; Leemann et al. , 2016). Therefore, Loghat 
Kami proposes AI-assisted preliminary screening followed by expert verification to ensure the 
reliability of contributed Kedah Malay dialect data. 
 
 
2.5  ChatGPT 
 

 
 
 PAGE 8 
 
 
Figure 2.5: ChatGPT Home Interface 
 
ChatGPT is a generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) platform based on Large Language Model 
(LLM) technology that supports various language -related tasks  including translation, 
vocabulary explanations and text generation. Previous research has demonstrated the potential 
of pretrained language models for natural language understanding through fine-tuning (Devlin 
et al., 2019) meanwhile generative language models have shown capabilities in text generation 
and few shot learning (Brown et al., 2020). 
 
However, general -purpose language models are not specifically designed for authoritative 
documentation of regional dialects such as Kedah Malay. Linguistic resources remain unevenly 
distributed across languages, creating challenges for Natural Language Pro cessing (NLP) 
applications involving underrepresented language varieties (Joshi et al., 2020). Therefore, AI-
generated dialect information may require expert verification to ensure its contextual and 
linguistic accuracy.

3.0 PROBLEM STATEMENT 
 
 
The Kedah Malay dialect represents an important part of Malaysia's linguistic and cultural 
heritage, containing distinctive vocabulary, pronunciation and regional variations (Asmah Haji 
Omar, 2008). Although previous linguistic studies have examined differ ent aspects of the 
Kedah Malay dialect including phonology and lexical variations, these findings are often 
documented through individual research studies rather than continuously updated digital 
platforms (Norfazila Ab. Hamid & Rahim Aman, 2014; Harishon  Radzi et al., 2018). As a 
result, there remains a need for systematic digital documentation that supports the continuous 
collection and preservation of Kedah Malay vocabulary, contextual meanings and authentic 
audio pronunciation recordings. 
 
Traditional dialect documentation commonly relies on fieldwork, interviews  and direct 
observation which can require time, resources and researcher involvement. These methods may 
limit the scale and frequency of data collection, making it challenging to capture diverse dialect 
variations across local communities. Digital crowdsourcing offers an alternative approach by 
allowing community me mbers to contribute linguistic information directly through an 
accessible platform. Previous studies have demonstrated the potential of crowdsourcing to 
support large-scale linguistic data collection through community participation (Ardila et al., 
2020; Leemann et al., 2016). 
 
However, crowdsourced linguistic data presents challenges in maintaining accuracy, 
consistency and reliability. Contributions from different speakers may contain inconsistent 
spelling, unclear audio pronunciation, incomplete contextual meanings  or inaccurate dialect 
information. Without a systematic verification mechanism, these inconsistencies may reduce 
the quality of collected dialect data and limit its usefulness for linguistic research. Therefore, a 
structured validation process is necessary to ensure that community-contributed information is 
properly reviewed before being accepted into a verified dialect corpus. 
 
Furthermore, integrating Artificial Intelligence (AI) into dialect documentation presents 
challenges when processing regional linguistic variations. Pretrained language models have 
demonstrated the potential to support different Natural Language Processing  (NLP) tasks 
 
 
 PAGE 17 
 
through fine-tuning and few-shot learning (Devlin et al., 2019; Brown et al., 2020). However, 
NLP resources are unevenly distributed across languages, particularly among underrepresented 
language varieties (Joshi et al., 2020). Consequently, the effectiven ess of general -purpose 
language models for processing Kedah Malay dialect data cannot be assumed without suitable 
linguistic datasets and evaluation. This highlights the need for an integrated platform that 
combines community crowdsourcing, AI -assisted preliminary screening  and final linguistic 
expert verification to support the development of a reliable and systematically organized Kedah 
Malay dialect corpus.

5.0 PROJECT SCOPE 
 
This project focuses on developing Loghat Kami, a web -based digital platform for 
documenting and preserving the Kedah Malay dialect. The system is designed for local 
community contributors, language experts and public users. It includes a multimodal 
crowdsourcing module that enables users to submit di alect vocabulary, contextual meanings, 
example sentences and authentic audio pronunciation recordings. 
 
The system also integrates AI-assisted processing, using an adapted pretrained language model 
to support preliminary screening and refinement of submitted dialect data by checking spelling, 
pronunciation and language use. Submissions that are flagged as inconsistent or uncertain are 
sent to the expert verification dashboard where language experts examine, correct, approve or 
reject them before verified contributions are stored in a structured digital dialect corpus. The 
final decision regarding the accuracy and authenticity of each contribution remains the 
responsibility of language experts. 
 
The project is limited to the Kedah Malay dialect and focuses on developing a functional 
prototype with crowdsourcing, AI-assisted processing and expert verification features. The AI 
component adapts an existing pretrained language model  which the system does not include 
developing a general purpose Large Language Model (LLM) from scratch.

6.0 PROJECT SIGNIFICANCE 
 
Loghat Kami plays an important role in supporting the documentation and preservation of the 
Kedah Malay dialect as part of Malaysia's linguistic and cultural heritage. By integrating 
community crowdsourcing, Artificial Intelligence (AI) -assisted data processing and language 
expert verification, the project aims to provide a systematic and accessible approach to 
collecting and preserving authentic dialect information. The platform encourages community 
participation while supporting the development of a structured digital dialect corpus. 
 
For language experts and researchers, Loghat Kami provides a centralized platform for 
managing and verifying dialect contributions. AI -assisted preliminary screening can help 
identify potential inconsistencies in submitted linguistic data, while expert verification 
supports the accuracy and reliability o f documented dialect information. The verified corpus 
may also serve as a valuable resource for future linguistic studies and language technology 
development. 
 
For local communities and public users, Loghat Kami offers an opportunity to actively 
participate in preserving their linguistic heritage. Community members can contribute dialect 
vocabulary, contextual meanings and authentic audio pronunciation recordings, while public 
users can access verified information for learning and reference purposes. This approach 
promotes greater awareness and appreciation of the Kedah Malay dialect, particularly among 
younger generations. In terms of technology, Loghat Kami introduces an innovative workflow 
that combines multimodal crowdsourcing with AI -assisted screening and expert verification  
which can reduce the manual effort needed to review large amounts of community-contributed 
dialect data. 
 
Overall, Loghat Kami aims to contribute to the long -term preservation of the Kedah Malay 
dialect by combining community participation, AI -assisted processing and expert validation. 
The project supports linguistic research, improves accessibility to dialect resources  and 
encourages the preservation of regional language variations for future generations.

7.0 METHODOLOGY  
 
 
 
This project will adopt the Agile Software Development Methodology, which emphasizes 
iterative development, collaboration and continuous feedback to ensure that the system meets 
user requirements. Agile is suitable for Loghat  Kami because the crowdsourcing module, AI -
assisted screening and expert verification dashboard require repeated cycles of development, 
testing and refinement based on feedback from contributors and language experts. The six 
Agile phases also correspond to the main activities of design and development research, namely 
requirement analysis, system design, prototype development, testing, evaluation and 
documentation. 
 
The first phase, Requirement Gathering involves a literature review and an analysis of existing 
language documentation platforms such as  Pusat Rujukan Persuratan Melayu (PRPM), Kamus 
Dewan Perdana, the SEAlang Library, ChatGPT and Glosbe. This phase identifies the 
functional and non -functional requirements, target users and essential system functionalities 
needed for dialect data collection, AI-assisted screening and expert verification. 
 
The second phase, System Design  focuses on designing the system architecture, database 
structure and user interface. Entity Relationship Diagrams (ERD), Unified Modeling Language 
(UML) diagrams and user interface wireframes will be developed to illustrate the relationships 
and interactio ns between community contributors, dialect submissions, AI processing and 
expert verification. This phase also involves planning the dataset structure and database 
organization for storing Kedah Malay dialect data, audio recordings and verification status. 

 
 
 PAGE 22 
 
 
The third phase, Development consists of two main iterations. The first iteration develops the 
crowdsourcing modul including user registration, authentication and the submission of Kedah 
Malay vocabulary, meanings, example sentences and audio recordings. The second iteration 
integrates AI-assisted screening which uses an adapted pretrained language model to check 
spelling, pronunciation and language use and to provide suggestions for expert review. An 
expert verification dashboard will also be developed in this iteration which allowing language 
experts to review, edit, approve or reject submissions before verified entries are stored in the 
structured digital dialect corpus. 
 
The fourth phase, Testing, evaluates the functionality, accuracy and usability of Loghat Kami. 
Unit and integration testing will be conducted to ensure that all modules function correctly. 
The AI -assisted screening will be evaluated using suitable performance metrics based on 
expert-reviewed dialect data. User Acceptance Testing (UAT) will be  conducted with 
community contributors and language experts to assess usability and reliability, and the 
feedback will be used to improve the system. 
 
The fifth phase, Deployment, involves deploying the Loghat  Kami prototype in a controlled 
testing environment to demonstrate its main functionalities. The system will be made accessible 
to selected community participants and language experts for testing and evaluation. This phase 
ensures that all modules operate together within the proposed dialect documentation workflow. 
 
The final phase, Evaluation and Documentation, focuses on assessing whether Loghat Kami 
achieves its project objectives and meets user requirements. Feedback from community 
contributors and language experts will be analyzed to identify system strengths and areas for 
improvement. The development process, AI evaluation results, testing outcomes and overall 
findings will be documented in the final project report with recommendations for future work.
